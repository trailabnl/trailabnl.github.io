New publication in CMI!

June 12, 2026

The key to guideline-driven antibiotic stewardship is knowing which infection was actually being treated. That information, the indication, is what lets you connect how someone was treated (the prescription, the episode) to what the guideline recommends, and actually judge whether it was appropriate. Without it, you are mostly just counting prescriptions.

So how do you capture it? Martijn Siepel, together Suzanne Kuijpers and colleagues (Jan Prins, Martijn Schut, Kim Sigaloff, Reinier van Hest, Rogier Schade, and René Spijker) at Amsterdam UMC, we reviewed 157 studies to see how the indication for antibiotic treatment is identified in practice. Four approaches emerged, each with a catch.

Manual chart review is accurate, but it does not scale. ICD and billing codes are free and available everywhere, but they capture what gets billed rather than what got treated; one study found that 68% did not match the prescriber's own indication. Prescriber-entered indications, picked while ordering, capture intent, but the added effort and usability problems hurt accuracy. Rule-based or AI methods were attempted by almost nobody: just 3 studies out of 157.

That last category is the most striking. The reason a drug was started is usually sitting right there in the notes, in plain words. Reading notes at scale is exactly what large language models are good at now, and that is what we are working on. More soon.
